02 / The savings evidence

One reform. Different cost bases.

First-year, ongoing and equivalent annual savings answer different questions. Compare them on the same basis.

Why A$555m becomes A$414.56m

The article’s larger estimate includes a first-year cost that does not recur. On the default ten-year appraisal, A$555m in year one and A$393m in later years have the same present value as A$414.56m each year.

The resulting threshold at an assumed A$10m loss is 2.701 percentage points. Using A$555m as a recurring annual saving would instead give 3.616 pp.

04 / Evidence & reproducibility

Open assumptions. Traceable numbers.

The model identifies the harm the savings could absorb. It does not estimate how much harm the reform will cause.

The model

NPV = Σ [ St × α − N × Δp × L ] / (1 + r)t
Δp* = PV(realised savings) / [ N × L × A(T,r) ]

N is the number of affected companies; L is the social loss per extra failure; Δp is the additional annual probability entered as a fraction; α is the share of savings realised. A(T,r) is the sum of annual discount factors. All sums run from year 1 to year T.

Results display Δp in percentage points. For a one-year view, the threshold is simply that year’s realised saving divided by N × L.

What the model assumes

Each affected company faces the same incremental risk and expected loss. Expected harm is additive across company-years; firms remain exposed every year. No survival depletion, contagion, firm heterogeneity or endogenous response is modelled.

Compliance savings are treated as resource savings. Wider benefits, transfers, distributional effects and information costs outside the assumed failure loss are not valued. “Positive” means positive under these specified assumptions.

A$10m is an illustrative social loss, not a measured typical failure cost. The defaults are scenarios, not empirical estimates of the reform’s risk.

Numerical audit and source reconciliation
  1. Treasury benchmark. A$210m ÷ 1,535 = A$136,807.8176 per firm. At A$10m loss, 210 ÷ (1,535 × 10) × 100 = 1.3680781759 pp. Its reciprocal is one additional expected failure per 73.0952 company-years, or 21 across the cohort in a year. The official release says over A$210m.
  2. Earlier-cost reconstruction. First year: 1,535 × [A$36,950 + 5% × A$45,050] = A$60,175,837.50. Ongoing: 1,535 × [A$36,950 + 5% × A$36,950] = A$59,554,162.50. It implements the article’s approximately A$60m case using earlier Treasury unit costs and the material-risk share, with no climate cost assigned to the remaining firms. It is not an official estimate of the 2026 proposal.
  3. Mandala reconciliation. The report’s rounded financial and climate aggregates imply A$177m + A$34m + A$344m = A$555m in year one; A$177m + A$19m + A$197m = A$393m ongoing. At ten years and 7%, equivalent annual savings are A$414.556220m and the threshold is 2.700691987 pp. This reproduces Chart 2’s 2.70. The article’s original model workbook was not supplied; this is an explicit reconstruction.
  4. Source inconsistency retained. Mandala p. 33’s per-firm climate costs and its 5%/95% split do not fully reconcile to the displayed aggregate totals. This dashboard uses the published aggregates for that scenario, rather than silently replacing them with a different total. Rounded source totals also need not reproduce multi-year headlines exactly.
  5. Company count and scope. About 1,535 is an ASIC estimate cited in Mandala p. 43, not a current company census. Revenue and asset thresholds are inclusive; two tests are required. Moving below the size test does not guarantee the absence of all reporting duties.
  6. Historical comparison and policy judgement. Treasury’s earlier factsheet estimated about 2,200 companies would leave the large-company category in the 2019 reform. The article’s proposed three-year review is the author’s recommendation, not a stated condition of the 2026 draft.
  7. Interpreting the research. Minnis (2011) finds lower borrowing costs among audited private US firms. Kausar, Shroff & White (2016) study the signal conveyed by a voluntary audit choice among UK firms that continue receiving audits. Neither study estimates an Australian failure-risk effect from this proposal.

The broader debate

The 2026–27 Budget describes A$10.2bn in annual regulatory burden reduction once its agenda is fully implemented. Mandala’s 2026 report reiterates an estimated A$160bn Commonwealth compliance burden, or 5.8% of GDP. These are separate measures and cannot be interpreted as net welfare gains.
